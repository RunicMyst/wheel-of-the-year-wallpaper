Here's the Wheel of the Year wallpaper I've been building. It's a desktop background
that redraws itself every day to show where we are in the year: the current Sabbat,
the zodiac sign, the Old Norse and modern month, tonight's moon phase, and the
solstices and equinoxes on the day they happen.

Download:  <paste your Google Drive link here>

It's free, there's nothing to sign up for, and it doesn't need the internet once it's
on your computer. I'd love to know what does and doesn't work for you - that's really
why I'm sending it.


TO INSTALL (Windows 10 or 11, about two minutes)

1. Download the .zip file and save it somewhere you'll keep it - your Pictures folder
   or your desktop is ideal. Avoid putting it inside OneDrive: it makes a new picture
   every day, and OneDrive would upload every one of them.

2. Right-click the .zip and choose "Extract All...". Don't open anything from inside
   the zip window itself - unzip it first.

3. Open the unzipped folder and double-click  Install.cmd

   Windows will probably show a blue box saying "Windows protected your PC".
   Click "More info", then "Run anyway". That warning appears for anything that
   isn't from a big company; there's nothing unusual about this file.

4. A black window opens for a few seconds, your wallpaper changes, and you press a
   key to close it. That's it - it updates itself every night from then on.


A FEW THINGS TO KNOW

- Your background starts plain black. That's normal. The wheel is there, but the
  photo behind it comes from folders you fill yourself - drop pictures into
  backgrounds\Samhain, backgrounds\Yule and so on, one folder per Sabbat. Every
  picture you put in gets used, a different one each day.

- A gear icon appears on your desktop called "Wheel of the Year Settings". That opens
  the settings in Notepad. You can change the month names, the colours, add your own
  dates (birthdays, anniversaries), show moonrise and moonset times, and more. Every
  setting has an explanation next to it, and nothing you type can break it.

- There's a README.txt in the folder with the full details.

- To remove it: double-click Uninstall.cmd. It stops there and then. Your wallpaper
  stays until you pick a different one in Settings > Personalization > Background.


IF SOMETHING GOES WRONG

Send me two things and it can usually be fixed quickly:

  1. The file  build\log.txt  from inside the folder (it records every run).
  2. What you expected to happen, and what actually happened.

Don't worry about describing it in technical terms - "nothing happened when I
double-clicked Install" is exactly the kind of report that helps.

Thanks for trying it.
